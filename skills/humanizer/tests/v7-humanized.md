*(I laugh softly as you all scoot closer, nearly toppling the basket of blankets. I raise a finger, mock-stern, then let the smile win.)*

Alright, alright... but mind you listen properly. Some stories don't like to be rushed.

Now...

That silver button lay warm in my palm, warmer than the cocoa by the fire. I *knew* it, though I couldn't yet say how. And just then—oh, as if Christmas itself were keeping time—the church bell tolled once in the distance.

That's when it came back to me.

Old Mr. Pemberton.

Years before, when he was still strong enough to climb ladders and mend roofs, he'd lost his wife on Christmas Eve. She'd sewn buttons for the whole village, you see. Coats, uniforms, wedding dresses. After she passed, Mr. Pemberton stopped coming to church. Stopped coming to dinners. Stopped knocking on doors.

And that button in my hand?

It was hers.

I didn't even stop to fetch my coat. I wrapped a scarf around my head, tucked the button into my pocket, and stepped out into the snow. The storm had quieted, just enough to make the world feel like it was holding its breath.

Mr. Pemberton's cottage glowed faintly when I knocked. He opened the door slowly, surprise flickering across his lined face.

"I didn't think anyone would come," he said.

Neither of us spoke for a moment. Then I placed the button in his hand.

"She'd want you to have it tonight," I said.

Oh, my darlings... the way his face changed. He sat down right there on the step and wept—not loud, not dramatic. Just the kind of crying that washes dust off the soul. He told me stories I'd never heard. About laughter. About dancing in the kitchen. About love that doesn't end, no matter how quiet it becomes.

And when I finally stood to leave, the church bells began to ring.

Midnight.

Clear. Strong. Perfect.

Christmas had found its way again.

*(I lean back now, eyes soft, the firelight warm.)*

The next morning, there was a knock at *our* door. Mr. Pemberton stood there with fresh bread and a smile I hadn't seen in years. He came to Christmas dinner. And the year after that. And every year until he was called home.

As for the child with the blue lantern?

No one else ever saw them.

But sometimes—only sometimes—on a snowy Christmas Eve, if you listen near the back door, you might hear three soft knocks. Not asking to come in.

Just checking that someone still remembers how.

*(I pull the blanket tighter around you.)*

Now then, my little loves... off to bed. Christmas comes quicker when you sleep—and who knows what doors might knock in the night? 🎄✨
